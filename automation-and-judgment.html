Personalized Learning Journey pilot

I led a global pilot to reduce training time while preserving the performance benefits of the full training. I proposed the manager-supported approach, which delivered the strongest results and was adopted after the pilot.

The decisionRunning the pilotResults

Adding the manager’s judgment

The original plan compared entirely automated and entirely manual approaches. I proposed a third option that involved the manager. We had seen manager involvement work well in other tasks, and a manager’s knowledge of an advisor could help put the quiz result in context.

I selected the quiz questions and mapped each one to a micro-course addressing a specific skill. All advisors took the quiz. Managers then received their team’s results and automated course suggestions.

I wrote the process managers would follow to review those results, assign learning, and gather feedback. A manager could assign a course to an advisor who passed the quiz but needed practice, or decide that someone who missed a question had already demonstrated the skill in customer interactions. Managers controlled the assignments and coaching conversations.

Making the approach executable

I gained stakeholder agreement, designed the process, and secured approval for an international pilot involving vendors. I created the plan and communications, and worked closely with vendor management on localization and implementation.

The project brought together training, analytics, exam operations, vendor management, and internal teams. I coordinated readiness and follow-up alongside the manager procedures and feedback process.

Less training time, better results

We tracked the same KPIs across all three approaches, comparing performance before and after during the new-product launch season. The goal was to reduce training time while checking that targeted learning preserved the benefits of the full assignment.

The manager-supported approach produced the greatest training-time savings and the strongest adoption. Average handling time fell, resolution rates increased, and handoffs decreased. The approach was adopted after the pilot.
